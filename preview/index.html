01

THE VISION

Interactive worlds for embodied intelligence

For decades, AI has made remarkable progress in worlds where the rules are already known: games, software, and digital environments. But the physical world plays by a different set of rules. Objects move, collide, deform, and interact; a robot must understand not only what it sees, but also what can move, what can be manipulated, and what will happen when it acts. Learning all of this directly in the real world is slow, expensive, and difficult to scale. This is why real-to-sim-to-real learning has become such a compelling path for embodied intelligence: reconstruct the real world in simulation, learn through interaction at scale, and bring those capabilities back to physical robots.

Science fiction has long imagined intelligence learning inside artificial worlds. In The Matrix, Neo acquires new abilities through interaction in a virtual environment. The idea points toward a broader vision for embodied intelligence:

Embodied intelligence needs worlds it can interact with, not just worlds it can see.

Building such worlds, however, remains difficult. Visual realism alone is not enough: a reconstructed world must also support modification, interaction, articulation, and physical simulation. For embodied intelligence, reconstruction must therefore move beyond generating 3D assets toward building simulation-ready interactive worlds.

02

INTRODUCING NEOWORLD-3

Introducing NeoWorld-3

We introduce NeoWorld-3, an agentic framework for reconstructing simulation-ready interactive worlds from visual observations. While existing reconstruction systems have achieved impressive visual quality through direct prediction, reconstructing complex structures, interactions, and physical constraints remains challenging. The emergence of large vision-language models and coding agents creates a new opportunity to move beyond this paradigm.

NeoWorld-3 is designed around three core principles: simulation-ready reconstruction, coding-agent based world construction, and object-to-scene scaling. Simulation-ready reconstruction preserves the geometry, articulation, and physical validity required for downstream simulation. Coding-agent based reconstruction enables vision-language models to actively construct and refine worlds through tools. Object-to-scene reconstruction allows the same framework to scale from individual objects to complex environments.

03

A NEW PARADIGM

Reconstruction through coding agents

Most existing 3D reconstruction systems are built around direct generation: given an observation, the model predicts a final representation. This approach has driven tremendous progress, but the reasoning behind the reconstruction remains hidden inside the generated result. A mesh does not explicitly describe why an object has a particular structure, how its components are related, or how it should be modified when requirements change.

Humans do not design complex objects by directly predicting every geometric element. We construct them through a sequence of operations: defining components, establishing relationships, modifying parameters, testing results, and refining errors. The progress of coding agents provides an opportunity to bring this construction process into 3D reconstruction.

NeoWorld-3 therefore does not assume that models should directly predict the final world. Instead, vision-language agents can reason about reconstruction tasks, generate modeling programs, invoke tools, evaluate intermediate results, and continuously refine their outputs through NeoMCP and NeoSDK. Reconstruction becomes an interactive process of reasoning, construction, verification, and refinement: the model is no longer only generating geometry, but actively building an interactive world.

04

AGENTIC WORLD CONSTRUCTION

Agentic world construction with NeoMCP and NeoSDK

NeoWorld-3 is built around a hierarchical agentic architecture that enables reconstruction to scale from individual objects to complete interactive scenes. Rather than treating a scene as a monolithic generation task, NeoWorldStudio acts as a world-building agent that reasons about the overall environment, decomposes complex scenes into individual entities, assigns reconstruction objectives, and coordinates the reconstruction process.

Each object reconstruction task is handled by a specialized object-level sub-agent. These agents understand object structures, construct geometry, reason about part relationships, and prepare assets for interaction. By making object reconstruction an independent and reusable capability, NeoWorld-3 can compose complex scenes from multiple object-level reconstruction processes.

In this hierarchy, high-level scene understanding guides object-level construction, while object-level results refine the overall world representation. NeoMCP gives agents dynamic access to different modeling capabilities, while NeoSDK provides the underlying geometric, rendering, and optimization tools required for construction and refinement. Together, they connect high-level reasoning with execution across the reconstruction process.

05

DIFFERENTIABLE REFINEMENT

Combining intelligence with optimization

One challenge in reconstruction is that vision-language models are strong at understanding visual concepts and structural relationships, but precise geometric parameter prediction remains difficult. Even when a model understands what an object should look like, directly predicting highly accurate geometry from numerical parameters is challenging.

NeoWorld-3 addresses this limitation through Differentiable Refinement. The agent first constructs an initial reconstruction, then refines it through differentiable rendering. The rendered result is compared with the original observation, image similarity serves as the optimization objective, and parameters in the generated geometric representation are updated through gradient-based optimization.

This creates a natural division of labor between reasoning and optimization. The vision-language model provides semantic understanding, decomposition strategies, and reconstruction programs, while differentiable optimization provides the numerical precision needed for high-fidelity geometry. Rather than relying on prediction alone, NeoWorld-3 can continuously improve reconstruction through visual feedback.

06

PHYSICAL VALIDITY

Physical Validity by Design: Watertight Geometry and Articulation

For embodied intelligence, reconstruction quality is ultimately measured by interaction. A robot does not simply observe an object; it grasps it, moves it, collides with it, and changes its state. Small geometric errors can therefore lead to significant failures in simulation, making physical reliability a core requirement rather than an optional refinement.

NeoWorld-3 introduces dedicated geometry validation tools to address this problem, including a watertight geometry pipeline. Watertightness is not merely a mesh-quality requirement: it provides reliable boundaries for physical interaction. Without watertight geometry, collision detection and penetration checking can become unreliable. NeoWorld-3 achieves watertight reconstruction by removing self-intersecting regions and smoothing connections between object parts, producing consistent geometry and reliable interaction boundaries.

Beyond geometric validity, NeoWorld-3 prepares objects for articulation and simulation. The system reasons about object parts, establishes joint relationships, evaluates motion ranges, and repairs invalid configurations before deployment into simulation environments. This allows reconstructed assets to preserve not only appearance and geometry, but also the structure and motion needed for interaction.

Through these capabilities, reconstructed assets become interactive worlds suitable for embodied learning. They can be deployed into physical simulators for physics-based validation and agent training, while insights from simulation can feed back into future reconstruction. This connects reconstruction, physical validation, and embodied learning in a unified workflow.

09

LOOKING AHEAD

Looking ahead

The history of AI suggests that major capability shifts happen when models move beyond passive prediction and begin to reason, use tools, and take actions. We believe 3D reconstruction is entering such a transition. Coding-agent based reconstruction offers a new path forward: instead of simply generating a final mesh, agents can actively understand, construct, verify, and refine interactive worlds through continuous interaction with tools and environments.

NeoWorld-3 is a step toward this future. As foundation models improve, we aim to scale coding-agent based reconstruction from individual objects to complex real-world environments, turning visual observations into simulation-ready worlds that support learning, interaction, and physical validation. More broadly, we see this capability as a key layer in the embodied intelligence stack: connecting the physical world to simulation, and simulation back to real-world behavior. If this process can be made scalable, every real-world observation can become the starting point of a new training environment for intelligent agents.

Our long-term vision is to build the foundation for the next generation of embodied intelligence: A world where AI systems can not only perceive and understand, but also interact with and learn within it.
